About the Architect

Ian R. Graham

Ian R. Graham lives in Surrey Heath. He is not a politician, an academic, or a lobbyist, and DD&SA has no institutional sponsor, no funders, and no party alignment. It is the work of one resident who watched the same failure repeat under eleven Prime Ministers and both major parties, concluded that the pattern was structural rather than accidental, and set out to design what a structurally honest alternative would actually look like.

DD&SA is the result.

The Work

The DD&SA Corpus now runs to more than sixty constitutional instruments totalling over a quarter of a million words — covering assembly selection, safeguards, and coordination, and extending across justice, health, education, energy, housing, policing, and the economy. The framework draws on Aristotelian civic thought, the modern sortition literature, and the republican principle of non-domination, and it has been deliberately stress-tested against adversarial critique at every stage: the corpus includes its own threat register and hostile stress-tests, published alongside everything else.

Independence by Design

Independence is not a limitation of this project. It is the point. A framework proposing to remove careers, parties, and patronage from governance could not credibly be produced by any of them. Every document is free, every argument is on the record, and the DD&SA Foundation is designed around a Dissolution Covenant: the organisation is committed to dissolving itself once its work is done. Nobody involved in building this system is permitted to profit from governing it.

An Open Invitation
