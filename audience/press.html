For the press

The story, sourced

In one line: a complete, published replacement for Westminster — designed by one person, free to read, with its own off-switch built in.

Key facts you can verify

The corpus exceeds sixty published instruments and 265,000 words, including a full written constitution, a justice architecture, fiscal constraint instruments, and a formal removal mechanism (the Civic Recall & Reversibility Act). All of it is free at dd-sa.org — nothing is embargoed, members-only, or crowdfunded. The project is independent: no party affiliation, no institutional backing, no candidates, and no donations sought.

The designer, Ian R. Graham, holds no privileged position in the system he proposes — a founding rule the corpus calls the Structural Engineer Principle. If DD&SA were adopted tomorrow, he would live under it on exactly the same terms as everyone else.

Three framings that survive fact-checking

The jury paradox: Britain has trusted randomly selected citizens with liberty itself for centuries — DD&SA asks why the same principle cannot govern. The engineering claim: governance failure is structural, not personal, so replacing the people while keeping the structure changes nothing. The off-switch: unlike the settlement it would replace, DD&SA publishes the mechanism for its own removal.

What DD&SA is not

Not a party. Not a protest movement. Not anti-democratic — it extends the democratic principle past the ballot box. And not asserted as finished: the corpus publishes the mechanism for its own removal, and the project maintains a standing open challenge to academics and critics to break the design.
